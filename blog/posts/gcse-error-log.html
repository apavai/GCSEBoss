Start a Simple GCSE Error Log in Week One

A GCSE error log is not a diary of how bad you are at maths. It is a short note about the last question you missed, so you do not make the same miss twice.

Week one of Year 11 is the right time to start a GCSE error log. You have sat in a few lessons. You have had a first homework or a first class question that did not land. That is enough. You do not need a colour-coded system and you do not need to wait until mocks.

Some of you started Monday. Some of you walked in midweek after INSET. If you have not had a lesson yet in a subject, you have nothing to log for that subject. Wait. You are not behind a national clock.

What a GCSE error log actually is

Three lines. That is the whole thing.

  1. The question, or enough of it that you will recognise it later
  2. What you got wrong (one sentence)
  3. What you will try next time (one sentence)

That is a GCSE error log. It lives in a cheap notebook, the back of your exercise book, or a phone note if your school allows phones for that. It does not need tabs, stickers, or a template you downloaded at midnight.

You log mistakes from homework, class questions, and short quizzes. You do not log every tick. You do not rewrite the whole mark scheme. You write enough that tomorrow-you knows what to practise.

If you do not know why it was wrong yet, write "stuck on step 2" and take that line into the next lesson. A stuck sentence is useful. A blank page is not.

Why week one is enough

The pile is still small. That is the point.

In October the wrong answers start to look the same and you cannot remember which lesson they came from. In week one you can still name the lesson. That is when the log is cheap to keep.

You do not need five subjects on day one. Pick the subject where you already missed something. One page. Cross it off when you get a similar question right later.

This is not a verdict on the year. It is a shopping list of skills that need another pass. Treat it like that and it stays useful. Treat it like a shame folder and you will stop opening it.

How to fill a GCSE error log without making it a second homework

Do the actual homework first. Then, if you marked something wrong, add the three lines. Five minutes. Timer if you need one.

Do not turn the log into a rewrite of the whole worksheet. Do not copy the teacher's model answer word for word. Your words, short.

Useful "what went wrong" lines look like this:

  • I used the wrong formula
  • I misread the units
  • I stopped after the first mark
  • I mixed up the two definitions
  • I guessed the last step

Useful "next try" lines look like this:

  • Check the units before I calculate
  • Write the formula first
  • Underline the command word
  • Do one more of the same type without the book

Useless lines look like "I am bad at this" or "revise more". Those do not tell you what to open tomorrow.

If a parent asks what you are doing, you can show the three lines. You do not owe a hallway debrief on every subject.

One subject, not the whole timetable

Start with one. Maths, science, or whatever already produced a wrong answer this week.

When that page has five entries, you can open a second subject. If you start six subjects on Friday night, you will abandon the book by Monday.

Keep the log next to the homework, not in a separate "organisation" pile you never touch. The back of the exercise book works because it travels with the subject.

Phone rules vary by school. If phones stay in bags or lockers, paper wins. Do not build a system that only works when you can photograph the board.

What you do with the log later

Once a week, pick two entries and try a similar question. If you get it right, tick the entry. If you miss it again, update the "next try" line. That is the loop.

You do not need a Sunday two-hour review in September. You need the due homework, then one or two log lines that matter.

Mocks are not this week's job. May is not this week's job. The useful test for week one is: can you name the last thing you got wrong without opening Instagram? If yes, the log is working. If no, write the three lines tonight and stop.

When the same mistake shows up twice

That is the whole reason the log exists.

If "misread the units" appears three times in a fortnight, your next homework starts with a units check before any calculation. If "stopped after the first mark" keeps appearing in English, you practise reading the full question before you write.

The log is only useful if you act on a pattern. Circling the same line every week without changing how you start the next question is just collecting paper.

Tell the teacher the pattern in one sentence if you can. "I keep dropping the units" is a better conversation than "I do not get it." You still do the homework. The log just makes the ask shorter.

What you can ignore

A printed template with twelve columns. A new set of highlighters. A speech about how this year matters more. Comparing your page count with a friend's. Logging every tiny spelling slip unless that is actually the skill you keep missing.

Also ignore the urge to make the first page perfect. Messy and used beats neat and empty.

Start tonight if you already have one miss

Open whatever you got wrong this week. Three lines. Close the book.

If you have not had a wrong answer yet because you have barely sat in lessons, wait until you do. Then start. That is still week one for you.

A GCSE error log is a small habit. It only works if it stays small. Three lines after a miss, a tick when you fix a similar one, then leave it alone. That is enough for September. The rest of the year has time.
