How to Revise for Multiple Subjects Without a Colour Coded Grid

How to revise for multiple subjects is not a colour coded masterpiece. It is a boring rotation that keeps the ignored ones alive.

By the end of week three, the pile feels uneven. One subject gets all your evenings. Two sit untouched. The colour coded grid looks like a solution and usually becomes another delay. You do not need nine perfect columns for how to revise for multiple subjects. You need a rule you will actually follow tonight.

How to revise for multiple subjects this evening

Use three rules.

  • Pick two subjects only.
  • Start with the one you have been avoiding.
  • Twenty minutes on that one counts as a real session.

Then take the second subject. Stop. Put the books away.

That is the whole habit. It scales across the week without a wall chart.

Why the colour coded grid stalls you

Grids feel productive. You choose pens. You redraw the week. You still have not opened the chemistry book.

A grid is a plan for when you already know how to start. Most Year 11 evenings are not that organised. They are tired, short, and full of the subject you like because it feels safer.

Rotation beats decoration. Two boxes tonight beat nine empty rainbow squares.

The trap: the subject you like gets everything

You will default to the easy win. English reading. A maths topic you already get. The history chapter you enjoy.

Name the trap out loud. Then put the avoided subject first for twenty minutes. After that, you can have the favourite as the second block.

If you reverse the order, the avoided one never happens. That is how uneven piles grow.

What twenty minutes is for

Twenty minutes is not a full past paper. It is a closed book question, a short method, a page of notes you can quiz from, or one stuck homework line.

It is long enough to restart a subject that went cold. It is short enough that you cannot invent a reason to wait for the "right" evening.

If you finish early, stop. Do not stretch it into a guilt marathon.

A simple weekly shape without a timetable rebuild

You already have a school timetable. Do not rebuild it as art.

Across five school nights, aim for each core subject at least twice, and each other subject at least once. Use the two subject rule every night. Track it with ticks in a planner, not a mural.

Weekends: one longer block if you want, still starting with the coldest subject. Or rest. Rest is not failure.

How this differs from a revision timetable post

A timetable decides when. This post decides how you share attention when several subjects are live at once.

You are not building May. You are stopping September from turning into nine half started folders.

Subject flavours without five systems

Science: one method or definition, then one question without notes.

Maths: one method you slipped on in class, two short practice lines.

English: one poem or scene page, one analysis question.

Humanities: one cause and consequence page, not a full essay every night.

Languages: ten minutes vocab or a short speaking drill before you quit for the favourite subject.

Same rotation. Different content.

When homework already fills the evening

Homework comes first. Then one avoided subject for fifteen to twenty minutes if you have anything left.

If homework is three hours, the rotation waits. Do not punish yourself with a grid you cannot keep. Restart tomorrow with two subjects again.

When a subject feels too far gone

Start smaller than twenty minutes if you need to. Open the book. Read the last lesson title. Write one question you cannot answer yet. That is still a restart.

Ask the teacher which one page to rebuild first. One recovered topic beats a weekend of pretending you will "catch everything up".

Digital folders without becoming a slide dump

If your notes live on a laptop, the same rotation applies. Two subjects. Avoided first. A closed book question at the end.

Do not spend the session renaming folders and picking icons. That is the colour coded grid in another skin.

Group chats and "everyone else is ahead"

Other people's screenshots are not your plan. They often show the subject they like.

Mute the comparison for one evening. Run your two subject rule. Check in with a classmate only for a specific stuck question, not for a full anxiety tour of the timetable.

After a heavy test week

When one subject has eaten three nights, the others go cold. The fix is not a revenge all-nighter across every book.

Next two evenings: avoided subjects only in the first slot. Let the tested subject wait until it is no longer the loudest voice in your head.

Parents who ask why it looks messy

Show them the ticks. Two subjects. Avoided first. Done.

Messy and finished beats neat and imaginary. If they want a longer plan later in the year, that is a different conversation. Tonight is triage.

What not to do

Do not colour code every subject before you open a book.

Do not promise four hours because a friend did.

Do not turn this into a May countdown. Summer 2027 is still far out.

Do not shame yourself for liking one subject more. Just stop letting it steal the whole week.

A script for the desk

"Two subjects. Avoided one first. Twenty minutes. Then the other. Stop."

Write it on a sticky note if you need to. Then start the cold book before your phone comes back.

Why week three is the right moment

Earlier in September you were still learning where rooms were. Now the load is real and the habits stick.

If you only feed the favourite subject until Christmas, January mocks feel like five subjects arriving at once. Rotation now is cheaper than panic later. It is still not a May countdown. It is maintenance.

Keep the pile moving, then sleep

How to revise for multiple subjects in week three is rotation, not rainbow planning. Two subjects an evening. Avoided subject first. Twenty minutes counts. Keep the pile moving, then sleep.
